      FCE — Functional Cognition & Execution

      About FCE

      FCE (Functional Cognition & Execution) is a fast, baseline-calibrated tool that measures how your cognitive and motor performance compares to your own normal capacity — not to population averages.

      "How are you functioning today compared to yourself?"

      What FCE measures

      FCE uses short, focused tasks to assess multiple domains of function. Examples include:

      • Reaction speed — time-to-response under standardized prompts
      • Inhibitory control — the ability to act / not act appropriately
      • Precision & motor control — accuracy under time pressure
      • Attention & task-switching — stability of focus across changes

      FCE is not an IQ test. It measures functional execution in the moment.

      Personal baselines, not population averages

      FCE becomes fair when it is calibrated. You build a personal baseline using several sessions performed when you are rested and functioning normally. Later checks compare your current performance to your own baseline profile.

      • People differ naturally (speed, coordination, style)
      • Devices differ (desktop vs mobile)
      • Inputs differ (mouse vs touch)
      • Context matters (sleep, stress, posture, distraction)

      FCE is designed to respect variance instead of punishing it.

      Context-aware by design

      Performance is context-sensitive. For that reason, baselines should be valid for the same context used to build them:

      • Same device type (e.g., phone vs laptop)
      • Same input method (touch vs mouse)
      • Same posture / environment when possible

      This reduces false conclusions caused by environmental change rather than true functional deviation.

      Integrity Protocol (anti-gaming)

      Any performance test faces the question: can someone manipulate results? FCE is designed with an Integrity Protocol as a core principle:

      • Baseline requires multiple sober sessions (not a single attempt)
      • Sessions can be checked for internal consistency and suspicious variance
      • Implausible baselines can be flagged as not trusted
      • Natural variability is treated carefully — the goal is harm reduction, not "catching" people

      The purpose is to reduce misclassification and prevent "false baselines" from becoming the standard.

      Thresholds (how deviation is interpreted)

      FCE does not rely on one universal cutoff. Instead, it looks for meaningful deviation from your baseline, such as:

      • Repeated drops across key measures
      • Deviations exceeding your normal day-to-day variability
      • Patterns consistent with fatigue / impairment (not one unlucky click)

      FCE results are signals, not labels.

      Unclear results are handled safely

      Real life has noise: poor sleep, stress, illness, pain, distraction. FCE treats uncertainty carefully:

      • Inconclusive ≠ failure
      • Variability ≠ dysfunction
      • A low result means: pause, assess context, retest — not a verdict

      Profiles (Drive / Work / Rehab)

      Different contexts require different safety priorities:

      • Drive profile: reaction time, divided attention, inhibition
      • Work profile: precision, sustained attention, error control
      • Rehab / NAV profile: trend tracking and recovery over time

      Same baseline logic — different risk framing.

      What FCE is not

      • Not a diagnosis
      • Not a clinical assessment
      • Not a legal judgment
      • Not a measure of intelligence or worth

      FCE assesses functional performance under standardized conditions. That's it — by design.

      Privacy

      FCE can be implemented in a privacy-first way. A strong default is:

      • No accounts
      • No tracking
      • No server upload
      • Local-only storage on the user's device

      Clearing the browser data clears FCE data.

      Why FCE exists

      FCE is built around a simple, ethical stance:

      "Functional performance matters more than assumptions."

      By focusing on function rather than categories, FCE aims to support fairer decisions in safety-critical contexts.

      Norwegian Context

      This page explains how FCE (Functional Cognition & Execution) could fit Norwegian frameworks — without overclaiming what it is.

      "Measure function, not molecules."

      1) Traffic safety: impairment vs presence

      A core challenge in drugs-and-driving policy is the gap between:

      • Detectability (a substance can be present long after acute impairment)
      • Functional risk (reaction time, attention stability, error rate, judgment)

      FCE aims to provide a functional performance signal that supports safer decisions in the moment. It does not try to identify what caused the deviation — it measures whether deviation exists.

      Substance-agnostic by design: sleep loss, stress, illness, medication, alcohol, cannabis — the system measures function.

      2) Workplace safety: risk jobs and functional readiness

      Many roles require stable execution: transport, construction, logistics, healthcare, industrial work. A functional tool can help answer:

      • "Am I stable enough for a safety-critical shift today?"
      • "Did poor sleep or stress reduce my execution?"
      • "Is my performance trending up or down over weeks?"

      This supports modern safety logic: protect people by measuring performance, not by guessing causes.

      3) NAV / arbeidsevne: function-first without labels

      Work capacity discussions often get trapped in categories: diagnosis vs not-diagnosis, "sick" vs "not sick". FCE offers a complementary lens:

      • Track cognitive-motor function over time
      • Document recovery trajectory
      • Support gradual return-to-work plans
      • Identify which work conditions are realistic (high-focus vs low-focus demands)

      Boundary: FCE is not a diagnosis tool. It is a structured performance mirror.

      4) Institutional safety: non-negotiable boundaries

      For ethical deployment, FCE must encode limits clearly. FCE is:

      • Not diagnosis
      • Not a medical evaluation
      • Not a legal judgment
      • Not a guilt/innocence machine
      • Not a "one score decides your life" tool

      A safe framing:

      "FCE assesses functional cognitive–motor coherence under standardized conditions. It does not determine legal guilt, replace clinical judgment, or diagnose pathology."

      5) Fairness: personal baselines protect individual differences

      Population averages punish variance. FCE uses personal baselines so comparison is:

      • You today
      • vs
      • You when you are functioning normally

      This can protect people with stable but naturally different profiles — including older adults and neurodivergent users — if pilots include diverse data and bias checks.

      6) Anti-gaming: preventing false baselines

      A reasonable concern is: "Can someone practice or cheat to pass while impaired?"

      FCE addresses manipulation risk through layered safeguards:

      • Baseline requires multiple sober sessions
      • Consistency checks for implausible baselines
      • Integrity pattern detection (variance signatures, suspicious dips)
      • Retest logic and uncertainty handling

      No system is magic. The goal is: make manipulation difficult, and make misclassification rare.

      7) A realistic pilot path in Norway

      1. Voluntary pilot with research partners
      2. Validate accuracy, fairness, and bias risk across groups
      3. Build trust through transparency, audits, and clear boundaries
      4. Only then discuss broader integration

      8) The ethical spine

      FCE is designed around harm reduction:

      • Minimize false positives
      • Allow retesting and reversibility
      • Treat uncertainty as "pause + reassess", not punishment
      • Prioritize safety without stigma
      "FCE is a tool of discernment, not punishment."

      How to Use

      FCE is designed to be simple: build a personal baseline first, then run quick checks later. Your results are compared to your own normal performance — not other people.

      1) Choose your test

      • Reaction Time — measures response speed and consistency.
      • Go / No-Go — measures response speed and inhibitory control (don't click on NO-GO).

      Tip: Reaction Time is easiest to start with. Go/No-Go usually needs more trials to stabilize.

      2) Set trials

      • Reaction Time: start with 5–10 trials per session.
      • Go / No-Go: start with 20–30 trials per session.

      More trials = more stable results, but don't grind yourself into fatigue.

      3) Build your baseline (first time)

      1. Be rested, sober, and "normal."
      2. Use the same device + input method (mouse vs touch) you plan to use later.
      3. Click Add Baseline Session.
      4. Repeat until you have at least 3 baseline sessions (recommended 3–5).

      Baseline is stored locally in your browser (device-specific). If you switch device, your baseline won't follow.

      4) Run a functional check (later)

      1. Choose the same test you have baseline for.
      2. Click Run Functional Check.
      3. Review the status (e.g., "Within normal range", "Slightly below", "Significantly below").

      Interpreting results

      • Within normal range — performance matches your baseline signature.
      • Slightly below normal — some deviation (could be stress, fatigue, distraction, etc.).
      • Significantly below normal — larger deviation; consider rest and retest.

      Important: This is a prototype tool for self-checking and research direction — not a medical diagnosis. If you feel unsafe, don't drive/work just because a test looks "fine."

      Best practices

      • Test in a similar environment each time (lighting, noise, posture).
      • Avoid "warming up" with many runs before recording baseline (it can bias your baseline).
      • If you get a weird session (many misses / false starts), retake and use the cleaner session.
      • Use the baseline list to verify you're building consistency over time.

      Reset and baseline management

      • Reset clears the current session UI and stops a running session.
      • Clear baseline deletes saved baseline sessions for the selected test type.

      FAQ

      What is FCE?

      FCE (Functional Cognition & Execution) is a web-based tool that measures how you are functioning right now compared to your own normal performance.

      It focuses on function — reaction speed, control, precision — rather than substances, diagnoses, or population averages.

      Is this a medical test?

      No. FCE is not a medical or diagnostic tool.

      It does not diagnose conditions, determine fitness to drive, or replace professional judgment. It is a functional self-check designed for awareness, research, and future policy exploration.

      How is this different from reaction time websites?

      • FCE builds a personal baseline instead of comparing you to others
      • Results are interpreted relative to your own normal
      • Multiple cognitive domains are measured separately
      • Sessions are stored locally and shown transparently

      Many sites show a number. FCE explains what that number means for you.

      Why does my reaction time differ between tests?

      Different tasks measure different cognitive demands.

      • Reaction Time focuses on pure speed
      • Go / No-Go adds inhibition and decision-making

      It is normal for Go / No-Go to be slower or more variable — control and speed are not the same thing.

      Why do results differ between desktop and mobile?

      • Touchscreens introduce latency
      • Screen size and posture differ
      • Input method (mouse vs touch) changes precision

      This is why FCE baselines are device-specific. Always compare checks on the same device and input method.

      Where is my data stored?

      All data is stored locally in your browser using localStorage.

      • No accounts
      • No cloud sync
      • No data leaves your device

      Clearing browser storage or switching devices will remove your baseline.

      Can I cheat the test?

      You can try — but it usually shows.

      Inconsistent reaction times, excessive false starts, or abnormal variance tend to degrade baseline quality and are flagged in session feedback.

      FCE is designed to reward honest, calm participation, not gaming the system.

      What does "Within normal range" actually mean?

      It means your current performance falls within the expected variation of your personal baseline — usually within one standard deviation.

      It does not mean "safe", "fit", or "approved". Context always matters.

      Why build a baseline at all?

      Humans differ naturally. Speed, control, and precision vary by person.

      Comparing you to yourself is often fairer, more accurate, and more informative than comparing you to population averages.

      What is FCE ultimately for?

      FCE explores a different way of thinking about impairment:

      • Measure function, not substances
      • Respect individual differences
      • Increase awareness of cognitive state

      It is both a practical tool and a prototype for future systems.
